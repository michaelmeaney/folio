# Meridian Presentation Design System

Version: 0.1.0

## Lineage

Meridian is an original Folio executive-editorial system informed by high-contrast strategy communication, including McKinsey's publicly described visual identity. It does not reproduce McKinsey templates, marks, proprietary typefaces or protected assets.

## Principles

- Every slide has a governing message, not merely a topic title.
- Evidence sits close to the claim it supports.
- High contrast means intellectual hierarchy as well as visual contrast.
- Dense information is acceptable when structured; clutter is not.
- Charts are arguments. Annotation should explain the implication.
- Prefer editorial composition, rules and alignment over cards.

## Composition

Use a strict 12-column grid with narrow gutters. Headlines normally occupy 7-10 columns. Reserve a narrow margin or side column for source notes, implications, definitions and exhibit labels.

Typical structures: governing thought + exhibit; two-column argument/evidence; annotated chart; issue tree; comparison matrix; waterfall; executive summary; quantified callout.

## Typography

Use Georgia for major editorial headlines and Aptos for body, labels, charts and diagrams. Keep serif usage sparse enough that the deck remains analytical rather than literary. Numeric callouts may be large and sans serif.

## Colour

Deep ink-blue is the dominant foreground. White is the default canvas. Use accent blue to organise information and orange only for a genuinely important contrast or exception. Avoid broad decorative colour fields.

## Charts

Charts must state the takeaway in the title or annotation. Prefer direct labels over legends. Baselines, units, time periods and populations must be explicit. Use neutral context series and one highlighted series. Sources belong in a consistent footer zone.

## Diagrams

Issue trees, operating models and process diagrams use fine rules, strong labels and clear grouping. Avoid rounded SaaS cards. Use whitespace, indentation and thin separators before containers.

## Photography

Use photography only when it carries evidence, human context or narrative value. Crop decisively. Do not use generic executive stock imagery.

## Prohibited patterns

No faux consulting logos, proprietary brand fonts, decorative chevrons, rainbow category palettes, giant card grids, unlabeled charts, weak topic-only titles, or text shrunk below the token minimum.
